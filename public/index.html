04

Research programme

Software, protocols, and products.

The programme studies security across abstraction layers: from implementation defects in native code to protocol state and observable behaviour on deployed connected products.

I

Native software

Source-led analysis and fuzzing of libraries, parsers, clients, and kernel subsystems, with current-version reproduction and matched controls.

II

Protocol platforms

Stateful and over-the-air testing of authentication, commissioning, session, and cross-layer behaviour. Thread and Matter are current doctoral research settings.

III

Connected products

Controlled black-box evaluation on physical products, linking protocol-level stimuli to repeatable device and application-level observations.

Scope note. Programme descriptions identify research directions. They do not imply a vulnerability result; findings enter the public record only after validation, attribution review, and disclosure.

05

Research method

From system model to reproducible evidence.

The same evidence discipline is used for source-level, networked, and physical-device experiments.

  1. 01

    Define the system

    Map interfaces, trust boundaries, ownership, protocol states, and observable outcomes before constructing experiments.

    model
  2. 02

    Reach the relevant state

    Preserve valid API, authentication, commissioning, and protocol sequences while varying the conditions under study.

    experiment
  3. 03

    Measure the consequence

    Use instrumentation, native builds, repeated trials, and matched controls to separate defects from harness artefacts.

    evidence
  4. 04

    Establish novelty

    Compare current source, history, advisories, issue trackers, and related literature before assigning discovery credit.

    context
  5. 05

    Coordinate remediation

    Report the observed behaviour, conditions, reproducible evidence, and tested fix direction through the project’s chosen route.

    disclosure

Taxonomy protocol

Evidence-led CWE classification.

CWE —published —
Primary

The earliest demonstrated root cause is mapped to the most specific MITRE entry supported by the public source and retained experiment.

Consequence

A second CWE appears only when a downstream weakness was observed independently; confidentiality and availability effects remain impact statements.

Uncertainty

When the evidence cannot support a specific mapping, the record says pending classification. Private aggregate records are not classified publicly.

IDs, official titles, and mapping eligibility are checked during every build against a locally pinned subset of the MITRE CWE catalog. The source archive, version, publication date, and SHA-256 are retained with the validator. MITRE root-cause mapping guidance ↗

Research standards

Reproducibility, attribution, and scope.

01

Reproducible evidence

Records identify the tested version, experimental boundary, controls, and observable result.

02

Traceable attribution

Independent rediscoveries remain validation studies and cite the earlier public report or fix.

03

Calibrated claims

Each entry states what was observed, the conditions under which it occurred, and what remains uncertain.
